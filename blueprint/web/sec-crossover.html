8 Crossover, Weak Harnack, Harnack, and Hölder Regularity

8.1 Crossover Estimate

Let \(d \in \mathbb {N}\), \(d \geq 3\), and \(E = \mathbb {R}^d\) throughout. Write \(B_{r}\) for the open Euclidean ball of radius \(r\) centred at the origin. Given a normalized elliptic coefficient \(A\) on \(B_{1}\) with ellipticity constants \(\lambda _A, \Lambda _A\) (with \(\lambda _A = 1\) after normalization, so \(\Lambda _A \geq 1\)), write \(\mathrm{IsSupersolution}{A}{u}\) for the assertion that \(u \in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\) satisfies \(-\operatorname{div}(A\nabla u) \geq 0\) in the weak sense, and \(\mathrm{IsSolution}{A}{u}\) for the corresponding equality.

The crossover estimate, due in this form to Moser, bridges positive and negative powers of a positive supersolution. Throughout this section we work with a fixed positive constant

\begin{equation*} C_{\mathrm{poinc}}= C_{\mathrm{poinc}}(d) {\gt} 0 \end{equation*}

denoting the dimensional Poincaré constant on the unit ball, a fixed constant \(M_{\mathrm{st}}{\gt} 1\) used in the smooth cutoff construction, and the dimensional John–Nirenberg constant \(C_{\mathrm{JN}}(d) {\gt} 0\).

Let \(\Omega \subseteq E\) be open, \(A\) an elliptic coefficient on \(\Omega \) with ellipticity constants \(\lambda , \Lambda \), \(u \in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega )\) positive on \(\Omega \) with \(\mathrm{IsSupersolution}A u\), and let \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty (\Omega )\). Then for every \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\),

\begin{equation*} \int _\Omega \frac{\varphi ^2 \, |\nabla u|^2}{(u+\varepsilon )^2}\, dx \; \leq \; \frac{4\Lambda }{\lambda }\int _\Omega |\nabla \varphi |^2\, dx. \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

Set \(\psi := \varphi ^2/(u+\varepsilon )\). By the smooth chain-rule witness construction build_test_function_eps, \(\psi \) lies in \(H^1_0(\Omega )\), \(\psi \geq 0\), and its weak gradient satisfies almost everywhere

\begin{equation*} \nabla \psi = \frac{2\varphi \, \nabla \varphi }{u+\varepsilon } - \frac{\varphi ^2\, \nabla u}{(u+\varepsilon )^2}. \end{equation*}

Since \(u\) is a supersolution and \(\psi \geq 0\),

\begin{equation*} 0 \; \leq \; \int _\Omega \langle A\nabla u, \nabla \psi \rangle \, dx \; =\; \int _\Omega \frac{2\varphi }{u+\varepsilon }\langle A\nabla u, \nabla \varphi \rangle \, dx \; -\; \int _\Omega \frac{\varphi ^2}{(u+\varepsilon )^2} \langle A\nabla u, \nabla u\rangle \, dx. \end{equation*}

Set

\begin{align*} Q & := \int _\Omega \frac{\varphi ^2}{(u+\varepsilon )^2}\langle A\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle \, dx, \\ X & := \int _\Omega \frac{\varphi }{u+\varepsilon }\langle A\nabla u,\nabla \varphi \rangle \, dx. \end{align*}

The supersolution inequality reads \(Q \leq 2X\). By Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the bilinear form \(\langle A\, \cdot ,\cdot \rangle \),

\begin{equation*} X^2 \; \leq \; Q \cdot \int _\Omega \langle A\nabla \varphi ,\nabla \varphi \rangle \, dx \; \leq \; Q \cdot \Lambda \int _\Omega |\nabla \varphi |^2\, dx. \end{equation*}

Combining \(Q \leq 2X\) with \(X^2 \leq Q\, \Lambda \int |\nabla \varphi |^2\), the elementary inequality \(Q^2 \leq 4QP\) implies \(Q \leq 4P\) where \(P := \Lambda \int _\Omega |\nabla \varphi |^2\) (see log_gradient_algebraic_conclusion). Hence

\begin{equation*} \lambda \int _\Omega \frac{\varphi ^2|\nabla u|^2}{(u+\varepsilon )^2}\, dx \; \leq \; Q \; \leq \; 4\Lambda \int _\Omega |\nabla \varphi |^2\, dx, \end{equation*}

and dividing by \(\lambda {\gt} 0\) gives the claim.

Under the same hypotheses, with \(u {\gt} 0\) on \(\Omega \),

\begin{equation*} \int _\Omega \frac{\varphi ^2\, |\nabla u|^2}{u^2}\, dx \; \leq \; \frac{4\Lambda }{\lambda }\int _\Omega |\nabla \varphi |^2\, dx. \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

Apply Theorem 8.3 with \(\varepsilon _n = 1/(n+1)\). The integrand

\begin{equation*} g_n(x) := \varphi (x)^2|\nabla u(x)|^2/(u(x)+\varepsilon _n)^2 \end{equation*}

increases monotonically as \(\varepsilon _n \downarrow 0\) to \(g_0(x) := \varphi (x)^2|\nabla u(x)|^2/u(x)^2\) at every point of \(\Omega \) since \(u {\gt} 0\) there. By Fatou’s lemma,

\begin{equation*} \int _\Omega g_0\, dx \; \leq \; \liminf _{n\to \infty }\int _\Omega g_n\, dx \; \leq \; \frac{4\Lambda }{\lambda }\int _\Omega |\nabla \varphi |^2\, dx. \end{equation*}

Let \(A\) be normalized elliptic on \(B_{1}\), \(u {\gt} 0\) a supersolution. Then for \(v := v_\varepsilon \),

\begin{equation*} \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/2}} \Bigl|v(x) - \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/2}} v\Bigr|\, dx \; \leq \; c_{\mathrm{BMO}}(d)\, \Lambda _A^{1/2}. \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

Pick a cutoff \(\eta \in C_c^\infty (B_{3/4})\) with \(\eta = 1\) on \(B_{1/2}\), \(0 \leq \eta \leq 1\), and \(|\nabla \eta | \leq 8M_{\mathrm{st}}\) (existence given by Cutoff.exists). Theorem 8.3 (with \(\varphi = \eta \), \(\lambda _A = 1\)) yields

\begin{equation*} \int _{B_{1/2}} |\nabla v_\varepsilon |^2\, dx \; \leq \; \int _{B_{1}} \eta ^2 \frac{|\nabla u|^2}{(u+\varepsilon )^2}\, dx \; \leq \; 4\Lambda _A \int _{B_{1}}|\nabla \eta |^2\, dx \; \leq \; 4\Lambda _A \cdot (8M_{\mathrm{st}})^2 \mathrm{vol}(B_{1}). \end{equation*}

Hence \(\| \nabla v_\varepsilon \| _{L^2(B_{1/2})} \leq 16M_{\mathrm{st}}\, \Lambda _A^{1/2}\, \mathrm{vol}(B_{1})^{1/2}\). By the Poincaré–Wirtinger inequality on \(B_{1/2}\) with constant \(C_{\mathrm{poinc}}(d) \cdot (1/2)^{1-d/2}\) (the dimensional rescaling factor),

\begin{multline*} \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/2}}\Bigl|v_\varepsilon - \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/2}} v_\varepsilon \Bigr|\, dx \; \leq \; \mathrm{vol}(B_{1/2})^{-1/2} C_{\mathrm{poinc}}(d) (1/2)^{1-d/2} \| \nabla v_\varepsilon \| _{L^2(B_{1/2})} \\ \; \leq \; \mathrm{vol}(B_{1})^{-1/2}C_{\mathrm{poinc}}(d) (1/2)^{1-d/2}\cdot 8M_{\mathrm{st}}\, \Lambda _A^{1/2}\, \mathrm{vol}(B_{1})^{1/2} \; =\; c_{\mathrm{BMO}}(d)\, \Lambda _A^{1/2}. \end{multline*}

The same statement holds on every ball \(B(x_0, R/2)\) with \(\overline{B(x_0,R)} \subset B_{1}\), by affine rescaling \(\texttt{rescaleToUnitBall}\) (see regularizedLog_ball_meanOscillation).

With \(p := c'(d)/\Lambda _A^{1/2}\), \(v := v_\varepsilon \), and \(a := \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/48}} v\, dz\),

\begin{equation*} \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/2}} \exp (p\, |v(z) - a|)\, dz \; \leq \; K_{1/2}(d). \end{equation*}

Apply the local John–Nirenberg lemma at scale \(\rho = 1/48\) on a finite cover of \(B_{1/2}\) by balls \(B(x_i, 1/48)\) with \(\# \{ x_i\} \leq \lceil 97^d\rceil \). \begin{equation*} \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B(x_i,1/48)} \exp \bigl(p\, |v - \langle v\rangle _{B(x_i,1/48)}|\bigr)\, dz \; \leq \; 1 + C_{\mathrm{JN}}(d). \end{equation*}

  • DeGiorgi.crossover_product_bound_exists

There exists a constant \(C(d) \geq 1\) such that for every normalized elliptic \(A\) on \(B_{1}\), every positive supersolution \(u\), and \(p_0 := c'(d)/\Lambda _A^{1/2}\),

\begin{equation*} \Bigl(\mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/2}} u^{p_0}\, dx\Bigr)\Bigl(\mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/2}} u^{-p_0}\, dx\Bigr) \; \leq \; C(d). \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

By a regularization step \(u \mapsto u + \varepsilon \), it suffices to bound

\begin{equation*} J_\varepsilon := \Bigl(\mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/2}}(u+\varepsilon )^{p_0}\Bigr) \Bigl(\mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/2}}(u+\varepsilon )^{-p_0}\Bigr) \end{equation*}

uniformly in \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) by \(K_{1/2}(d)^2\). With \(v_\varepsilon = -\log (u+\varepsilon ) + \log \varepsilon \), \(a := \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/48}} v_\varepsilon \), \(F_\pm (x) := \exp (\pm p_0(v_\varepsilon - a))\) and \(F_{\mathrm{abs}} := \exp (p_0|v_\varepsilon - a|)\), we have \(F_\pm \leq F_{\mathrm{abs}}\) pointwise. Theorem 8.7 together with \(\texttt{setAverage\_ mono\_ of\_ nonneg\_ local}\) yields

\begin{equation*} \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/2}} F_+\, dx \leq K_{1/2}(d), \qquad \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1/2}} F_-\, dx \leq K_{1/2}(d). \end{equation*}

Now \((u+\varepsilon )^{p_0} = \exp (p_0(\log \varepsilon - a))\, F_+\) and \((u+\varepsilon )^{-p_0} = \exp (p_0(a - \log \varepsilon ))\, F_-\) pointwise on \(B_{1/2}\). The two scalar prefactors multiply to \(1\) (cancellation \(\texttt{hcancel}\)), so

\begin{equation*} J_\varepsilon \; =\; \Bigl(\mathop{⨍}\nolimits F_+\Bigr)\Bigl(\mathop{⨍}\nolimits F_-\Bigr) \; \leq \; K_{1/2}(d)^2. \end{equation*}

Letting \(\varepsilon \downarrow 0\) along \(\varepsilon _n = 1/(n+1)\), the shifted integrals converge to the original ones by dominated/monotone convergence (the integrability of \(|u|^{p_0}\) is supplied by the \(W^{1,2}\) estimate using \(p_0 {\lt} 1 \leq 2\), see hpow_int). Choose \(C(d) := \max \{ 1, K_{1/2}(d)^2\} \).

\(0 {\lt} p_0(A) {\lt} 1\), \(p_0(A) \leq c'(d)\), and \(\Lambda _A\, p_0(A)^2 = c'(d)^2\).

Proof ▼

Since \(\Lambda _A \geq 1\), \(\Lambda _A^{1/2} \geq 1\), so \(0 {\lt} p_0 = c'(d)/\Lambda _A^{1/2} \leq c'(d) {\lt} 1\). The identity follows from \((c'(d)/\Lambda _A^{1/2})^2 \cdot \Lambda _A = c'(d)^2\).

The proof of the weak Harnack inequality chains three pieces at half scale. We rescale \(v(z) := u(z/2)\) from \(B_{1/2}\) to \(B_{1}\) via the rescaled coefficient \(A' := \texttt{rescaledCoeff}\, A\) which preserves \(\Lambda \). The three steps are:

  • Forward Moser at level \(p_0\) (weak_harnack_stage_one_forward_ball):

    \begin{equation*} \Bigl(\int _{B_{1/4}} u^{q\, d/(d-2)}\Bigr)^{p_0(d-2)/(qd)} \leq C_{\mathrm{wH},0\to }(d)\, (\Lambda _A p_0^2/(1-q)^2 + 1)^{d\chi /2} \int _{B_{1/2}} u^{p_0}. \end{equation*}

  • Crossover (Corollary 8.11) at the level \(p_0\):

    \begin{equation*} \Bigl(\int _{B_{1/2}} u^{p_0}\Bigr)\Bigl(\int _{B_{1/2}} u^{-p_0}\Bigr) \leq C_{\mathrm{cross}}(d)\, \mathrm{vol}(B_{1/2})^2. \end{equation*}

  • Inverse Moser (weak_harnack_stage_one_inverse_ball):

    \begin{equation*} (\Lambda _A p_0^2 + 1)^{-d/2}\Bigl(\int _{B_{1/2}} u^{-p_0}\Bigr)^{-1} \leq C_{\mathrm{wH},0}(d)\, \bigl(\operatorname*{ess\, inf}_{B_{1/4}} u\bigr)^{p_0}. \end{equation*}

Multiplying these three estimates yields the chain.

Remark 8.30
#

The dependence on the ellipticity bound \(\Lambda _A\) enters Holder regularity in two ways: through the multiplicative prefactor \(\Lambda _A^{d/(2p_0)}\) inherited from the forward Moser bound, and through the Holder exponent \(\alpha (A) = \exp (-C_{\mathrm{Har}}(d)\, \Lambda _A^{1/2})\) inherited from the Harnack constant. As \(\Lambda _A \to \infty \), \(\alpha (A) \to 0\) exponentially, quantifying the well-known degeneration of De Giorgi–Nash–Moser regularity for highly anisotropic elliptic operators.
