4 Ball Extension, Approximation, and Supporting Tools

This section collects the analytic infrastructure underlying the De Giorgi development: a Sobolev chain rule on open sets, smooth approximation of \(W^{1,p}\) functions on the unit ball, an explicit unit-ball extension operator together with its \(L^p\) and \(W^{1,p}\) estimates, the positive-part machinery, and a small toolkit for \(L^p\) limits, finite covers, and pointwise component analysis. Throughout we work in the Euclidean space \(E := \mathbb {R}^d\) with \(d \ge 1\).

4.1 Sobolev chain rule

The first goal is the chain rule for compositions \(\Phi \circ u\) when \(u\) is a \(W^{1,2}\) function and \(\Phi \) is a smooth scalar function with bounded derivative.

Let \(u \in W^{1,2}(B_{1})\) with weak gradient \(\nabla u\) packaged as a MemW1pWitness, let \(i \in \{ 1,\dots ,d\} \), and let \(\Phi \in C^{\infty }(\mathbb {R})\) satisfy \(\Phi (0)=0\) and \(|\Phi '(t)| \le M\) for all \(t \in \mathbb {R}\). Then \(\partial _i \Phi (u) = \Phi '(u)\, \partial _i u\) in the weak sense on \(B_{1}\).

Proof ▼

Let \(M\) be the prescribed bound on \(|\Phi '|\), so \(M \ge 0\). Since \(B_{1}\) has finite Lebesgue measure, by exists_smooth_W12_approx_on_unitBall there is a sequence \(\psi _n \in C^{\infty }_c(\mathbb {R}^d)\) with

\begin{align*} \mathrm{eLpNorm}{\psi _n - u}_{L^2(B_{1})} & \to 0,\\ \mathrm{eLpNorm}{\partial _i \psi _n - \partial _i u}_{L^2(B_{1})} & \to 0. \end{align*}

Passing to a subsequence (still denoted \(\psi _n\)) we may also assume \(\psi _n \to u\) a.e. on \(B_{1}\) via exists_ae_tendsto_of_eLpNorm_tendsto.

Since each \(\psi _n\) is smooth and compactly supported, the classical chain rule and integration by parts yield, for every test function \(\varphi \in C^{\infty }_c(B_{1})\),

\begin{multline*} \int _{B_{1}} \Phi (\psi _n)\, \partial _i \varphi \, dx = -\int _{B_{1}} \Phi ’(\psi _n)\, \partial _i \psi _n\, \varphi \, dx. \end{multline*}

We pass to the limit on each side.

Left-hand side. The function \(\Phi \) is \(M\)-Lipschitz because \(\Phi '\) is bounded and \(\Phi \) is differentiable. Hence \(|\Phi (\psi _n(x)) - \Phi (u(x))| \le M\, |\psi _n(x)-u(x)|\) pointwise, so

\begin{equation*} \mathrm{eLpNorm}{\Phi (\psi _n)-\Phi (u)}_{L^2(B_{1})} \le M\, \mathrm{eLpNorm}{\psi _n - u}_{L^2(B_{1})} \to 0. \end{equation*}

The function \(\partial _i \varphi \) lies in \(L^2(B_{1})\) (it is bounded and compactly supported), so by Hölder’s inequality \(\int _{B_{1}} \Phi (\psi _n)\, \partial _i \varphi \, dx \to \int _{B_{1}} \Phi (u)\, \partial _i\varphi \, dx\).

Right-hand side. Decompose

\begin{equation*} \Phi ’(\psi _n)\, \partial _i \psi _n - \Phi ’(u)\, \partial _i u = \Phi ’(\psi _n)\, (\partial _i \psi _n - \partial _i u) + (\Phi ’(\psi _n) - \Phi ’(u))\, \partial _i u. \end{equation*}

The first term is bounded in \(L^2\)-norm by \(M\, \mathrm{eLpNorm}{\partial _i \psi _n - \partial _i u}_{L^2(B_{1})}\to 0\). For the second term, \(\Phi '\) is continuous and \(\psi _n \to u\) a.e., so \(\Phi '(\psi _n) \to \Phi '(u)\) a.e. with uniform bound \(M\). The dominated convergence theorem applied to \(|\Phi '(\psi _n) - \Phi '(u)|^2 |\partial _i u|^2\) (dominated by \(4M^2 |\partial _i u|^2 \in L^1\)) yields convergence of the second term in \(L^2\). Multiplying by \(\varphi \in L^\infty _c\) and integrating gives convergence of the right-hand side to \(-\int _{B_{1}} \Phi '(u)\, \partial _i u\, \varphi \, dx\). The chain-rule identity follows.

It now suffices to verify the weak derivative identity locally, that is, on each ball \(B_{r}(x_0) \subseteq \Omega \) (HasWeakPartialDeriv’_of_local). 4.2 Smooth approximation on the unit ball

We now produce smooth, compactly supported approximations of any \(W^{1,p}\) function on the unit ball, with control on both function and gradient errors. The construction proceeds in three layers: (i) a Lipschitz cutoff profile \(\eta _{r,R}\), (ii) inward dilation, and (iii) convolution with a mollifier.

(eLpNorm_pi_le_sum_component, eLpNorm_pi_component_le, aestronglyMeasurable_pi_component, aestronglyMeasurable_pi_of_components, memLp_pi_component)
For \(F : \alpha \to \mathbb {R}^d\) and \(1 \le p \le \infty \):

  1. \(\mathrm{eLpNorm}{F}_{L^p} \le \sum _i \mathrm{eLpNorm}{F_i}_{L^p}\);

  2. \(\mathrm{eLpNorm}{F_i}_{L^p} \le \mathrm{eLpNorm}{F}_{L^p}\);

  3. \(F\) is a.e. strongly measurable iff each component is;

  4. \(F \in L^p\) iff each component is.

Proof ▼

For the first item, the pointwise vector-norm bound on \(\mathbb {R}^d\) reads \(\| F(x)\| \le \sum _i \| F(x)_i\| \) (pi_norm_le_sum_norms); applying \(\mathrm{eLpNorm}{\cdot }_{L^p}\) and using monotonicity of the eLpNorm under pointwise inequalities of nonnegative functions (eLpNorm_mono_real) bounds \(\mathrm{eLpNorm}{F}_{L^p}\) above by \(\mathrm{eLpNorm}{x \mapsto \sum _i \| F(x)_i\| }_{L^p}\). Iterating the Minkowski triangle inequality eLpNorm_add_le for \(1 \le p \le \infty \) over the finite index set bounds this in turn by \(\sum _i \mathrm{eLpNorm}{x \mapsto \| F(x)_i\| }_{L^p}\), and then \(\mathrm{eLpNorm}{x \mapsto \| F(x)_i\| }_{L^p} = \mathrm{eLpNorm}{F_i}_{L^p}\) since the eLpNorm of \(\| h\| \) equals that of \(h\) (eLpNorm_norm).

For the single-component bound \(\mathrm{eLpNorm}{F_i}_{L^p} \le \mathrm{eLpNorm}{F}_{L^p}\), the pointwise inequality \(\| F(x)_i\| \le \| F(x)\| \) holds for any vector in \(\mathbb {R}^d\) (the \(i\)-th component is dominated by the sup norm, hence by any equivalent norm), and again \(\mathrm{eLpNorm}{\cdot }_{L^p}\) is monotone. Strong measurability componentwise follows by composition with the continuous projection \(\mathbb {R}^d \to \mathbb {R}\) (continuous_apply); the converse direction assembles the components into a measurable function via the product topology on \(\mathbb {R}^d\). Membership in \(L^p\) in either direction now follows from the two norm comparisons together with strong measurability.

Theorem 4.85

Let \(0 {\lt} r\), \(0 {\lt} \rho \), and \(r + 2\rho {\lt} R\). Then there is a finite set \(T \subseteq \overline{B_{r}(x_0)}\) such that

\begin{equation*} \overline{B_{r}(x_0)} \subseteq \bigcup _{c \in T} B_{\rho }(c), \quad \overline{B_{2\rho }(c)} \subseteq B_{R}(x_0) \text{ for all } c \in T. \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

The closed ball \(\overline{B_{r}(x_0)}\) is compact in the finite-dimensional Euclidean space \(E\) (isCompact_closedBall), and the family \(\{ B_{\rho }(c): c \in \overline{B_{r}(x_0)}\} \) is an open cover of \(\overline{B_{r}(x_0)}\) since each centre \(c\) is contained in its own ball of radius \(\rho {\gt} 0\) (Metric.ball_mem_nhds). By compactness (IsCompact.elim_nhds_subcover) we extract a finite subset \(T \subseteq \overline{B_{r}(x_0)}\) for which the union of the \(B_{\rho }(c)\) over \(c \in T\) still covers \(\overline{B_{r}(x_0)}\).

It remains to verify the buffered containment \(\overline{B_{2\rho }(c)} \subseteq B_{R}(x_0)\) for each \(c \in T\). Since \(c\) lies in \(\overline{B_{r}(x_0)}\) we have \(\operatorname{dist}(c,x_0) \le r\), and any \(x \in \overline{B_{2\rho }(c)}\) satisfies \(\operatorname{dist}(x,c) \le 2\rho \). By the triangle inequality

\begin{equation*} \operatorname{dist}(x, x_0) \le \operatorname{dist}(x, c) + \operatorname{dist}(c, x_0) \le 2\rho + r {\lt} R, \end{equation*}

where the strict inequality is exactly the buffer hypothesis \(r + 2\rho {\lt} R\). Hence \(x \in B_{R}(x_0)\), completing the proof.
