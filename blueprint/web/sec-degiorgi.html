6 De Giorgi Iteration and Local Boundedness

This section is the formal core of the De Giorgi \(L^2 \to L^\infty \) argument for non-negative subsolutions of a divergence-form linear elliptic equation \(\operatorname{div}(A \nabla u) \le 0\) on the unit ball \(B_{1} \subset \mathbb {R}^d\), with \(A\) measurable, symmetric, and uniformly elliptic with constants \(0 {\lt} \lambda \le \Lambda \) (here \(\lambda \) denotes always the global ellipticity constant, never a level threshold). The Lean development is organised into six layers, each realised by a dedicated module:
DeGiorgiIteration.CutoffAdmissibility, DeGiorgiIteration.Energy,
DeGiorgiIteration.PreIteration, DeGiorgiIteration.Recurrence, and DeGiorgiIteration.Linfty, all reassembled into the public interface DeGiorgiIteration and reexported by DeGiorgiTheory. We follow the same layering below.

Throughout the section \(E = \mathbb {R}^d\) with \(d \ge 1\), \(\Omega \subset E\) is an open set with finite Lebesgue measure, and \(A\) is an elliptic coefficient field with constants \(\lambda \), \(\Lambda \) encoded in EllipticCoeff; we write \(\mathrm{ellipticityRatio}(A) = \Lambda /\lambda \).

6.3 Weighted Caccioppoli inequality

We now turn to the analytical heart of the chapter: the weighted Caccioppoli inequality for truncations of subsolutions. We use the abbreviation

\begin{equation*} \nabla \eta (x) := (\partial _1 \eta (x), \dots , \partial _d \eta (x)) \end{equation*}

identified with the inner-product representation of the Fréchet derivative; see deGiorgiFderivVec. The norm \(\| \nabla \eta (x)\| \) then equals the operator norm \(\| D\eta (x)\| \), which is recorded as deGiorgi_norm_fderivVec_eq_norm_fderiv.

Lemma 6.14
✓
#

(Formalized as private declaration DeGiorgi.weighted_caccioppoli_pointwise.)

Let \(\Lambda {\gt} 0\), \(\eta , \zeta , E, M, v \in \mathbb {R}\), and assume the pointwise coercivity condition \(|M|^2 \le \Lambda E\). Then

\begin{align*} 2 \eta |v| \zeta |M| \le \tfrac {1}{2}\, \eta ^2 E + 2\Lambda \, \zeta ^2 |v|^2. \end{align*}
Proof ▼

Apply Young’s inequality \(2ab \le a^2 + b^2\) with \(a = \eta |M| / \sqrt{2\Lambda }\) and \(b = \sqrt{2\Lambda }\, \zeta |v|\) to get

\begin{equation*} 2 \eta |v| \zeta |M| \le \frac{\eta ^2 |M|^2}{2\Lambda } + 2\Lambda \, \zeta ^2 |v|^2. \end{equation*}

The hypothesis \(|M|^2 \le \Lambda E\) then yields \(\eta ^2 |M|^2 / (2\Lambda ) \le \tfrac {1}{2} \eta ^2 E\), completing the proof.

Theorem 6.15 Abstract absorption
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.weighted_caccioppoli_absorb

Let \((\alpha , \mu )\) be a measure space and let \(E, M, \eta , \zeta , v : \alpha \to \mathbb {R}\) be measurable functions with \(\Lambda {\gt} 0\). Assume the integrability conditions and:

  1. \(\int \eta ^2 E \, d\mu \le \int 2 \eta |v| \zeta |M| \, d\mu \),

  2. \(|M|^2 \le \Lambda E\) almost everywhere.

Then \(\int \eta ^2 E \, d\mu \le 4 \Lambda \int \zeta ^2 |v|^2 \, d\mu \).

Proof ▼

Apply the pointwise Young inequality almost everywhere, integrate to get

\begin{equation*} \int 2\eta |v|\zeta |M|\, d\mu \le \tfrac {1}{2}\int \eta ^2 E\, d\mu + 2\Lambda \int \zeta ^2 |v|^2 \, d\mu , \end{equation*}

combine with the assumed core estimate to obtain \(\int \eta ^2 E\, d\mu \le \tfrac 12 \int \eta ^2 E\, d\mu + 2\Lambda \int \zeta ^2 |v|^2 \, d\mu \), and absorb to conclude.

The PDE-facing weighted Caccioppoli inequality is the following statement, which is the analytical core of the De Giorgi argument.

  • DeGiorgi.caccioppoli_weighted_of_subsolution

Let \(\Omega \subset E\) be open with finite measure, \(A\) an elliptic coefficient field on \(\Omega \), \(u\) a subsolution (\(\texttt{IsSubsolution}\, A\, u\)), and \(\eta \) a smooth bounded nonneg function with \(|\eta | \le C_0\), \(\| \nabla \eta \| \le C_1\), \(\mathrm{tsupp}\, \eta \subseteq \Omega \). Let \(\ell \in \mathbb {R}\) and assume \((u - \ell )_+ \in W^{1,2}(\Omega )\) and that \(\varphi _{\eta ,u,\ell }\) admits a \(W^{1,2}_0(\Omega )\) structure. Then

\begin{multline*} \int _\Omega \eta (x)^2 \, \bigl\| \nabla (u-\ell )_+(x) \bigr\| ^2 \, dx \le \\ 4\, \frac{\Lambda }{\lambda }\, \int _\Omega \| \nabla \eta (x)\| ^2 \, \bigl|(u-\ell )_+(x)\bigr|^2 \, dx. \end{multline*}
Proof ▼

Test the subsolution inequality against \(\varphi := \varphi _{\eta ,u,\ell } = \eta ^2 (u-\ell )_+\), which is admissible by the cutoff admissibility theorem and is nonnegative. The subsolution condition gives \(\int _\Omega \langle A\nabla u, \nabla \varphi \rangle \, dx \le 0\). Computing the gradient of \(\varphi \),

\begin{align*} \nabla \varphi (x) = \eta (x)^2 \, \nabla (u-\ell )_+(x) + 2\, \eta (x)\, (u-\ell )_+(x) \, \nabla \eta (x), \end{align*}

which is the content of deGiorgiCutoffTestWitnessWeighted_grad. Combining with the identification \(\nabla u = \nabla (u - \ell )_+\) on \(\{ u {\gt} \ell \} \) and \(\nabla (u-\ell )_+ = 0\) on \(\{ u \le \ell \} \) (the lemmas truncGrad_eq_on_superlevel and truncGrad_zero_on_sublevel), we may rewrite

\begin{multline*} 0 \ge \int _\Omega \eta (x)^2 \, \langle A(x) \nabla (u-\ell )_+(x), \nabla (u-\ell )_+(x)\rangle \, dx \\ + \int _\Omega 2 \eta (x) (u-\ell )_+(x)\, \langle A(x) \nabla (u-\ell )_+(x), \nabla \eta (x)\rangle \, dx. \end{multline*}

By the coercivity bound for \(A\) (lemma \(\Lambda \)-coercive: \(\langle Aw,w\rangle \ge \lambda \| w\| ^2\)) and the upper bound (lemma \(\langle Aw,w\rangle \le \Lambda \| w\| ^2\) together with \(\| Aw\| ^2 \le \Lambda \langle Aw,w\rangle \)), the first term is bounded below by \(\lambda \int \eta ^2 \| \nabla (u-\ell )_+\| ^2\), while the second is dominated in absolute value by

\begin{equation*} \int _\Omega 2\, \eta (x) (u-\ell )_+(x) \, \| A(x)\nabla (u-\ell )_+(x)\| \, \| \nabla \eta (x)\| \, dx. \end{equation*}

Setting \(E := \langle A\nabla (u-\ell )_+, \nabla (u-\ell )_+\rangle \) and \(M := \| A\nabla (u-\ell )_+\| \), we have \(|M|^2 \le \Lambda E\), and applying the abstract absorption lemma above with these choices yields

\begin{equation*} \int _\Omega \eta (x)^2\, E(x)\, dx \le 4\, \Lambda \int _\Omega \| \nabla \eta (x)\| ^2\, |(u-\ell )_+(x)|^2 \, dx. \end{equation*}

Coercivity now bounds the left-hand side from below by \(\lambda \int _\Omega \eta ^2 \| \nabla (u-\ell )_+\| ^2\), and dividing by \(\lambda \) produces the announced inequality with constant \(4 \Lambda /\lambda \).

Theorem 6.19
✓

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.superlevel_measureReal_le_integral_posPart

Let \((\alpha , \mu )\) be a finite measure space, \(u : \alpha \to \mathbb {R}\), and let \(\theta {\lt} \ell \). If \(|(u-\theta )_+|^2\) is integrable, then

\begin{equation*} \mu \bigl(\{ x : \ell {\lt} u(x)\} \bigr) \le \frac{1}{(\ell -\theta )^2}\, \int |(u-\theta )_+(x)|^2 \, d\mu (x). \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

Set \(f(x) := |(u-\theta )_+(x)|^2\) and \(\varepsilon := (\ell -\theta )^2\). The hypothesis \(\theta {\lt} \ell \) ensures \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\). By positivePartSub_sq_ge_gap_sq_of_lt, on the superlevel set \(\{ x : \ell {\lt} u(x)\} \) the value of \(f\) is at least \((\ell - \theta )^2 = \varepsilon \), so we have the set inclusion

\begin{equation*} \{ x : \ell {\lt} u(x)\} \subseteq \{ x : \varepsilon \le f(x)\} . \end{equation*}

By monotonicity of measure (measureReal_mono) this gives \(\mu \{ \ell {\lt} u\} \le \mu \{ \varepsilon \le f\} \).

Markov’s inequality for nonnegative integrable functions (MeasureTheory.mul_meas_ge_le_integral_of_nonneg) applied to \(f\) at threshold \(\varepsilon \) yields

\begin{equation*} \varepsilon \cdot \mu \{ \varepsilon \le f\} \le \int _{\alpha } f(x)\, d\mu (x). \end{equation*}

Combining the set inclusion with monotone multiplication by \(\varepsilon \ge 0\),

\begin{equation*} \varepsilon \cdot \mu \{ \ell {\lt} u\} \le \varepsilon \cdot \mu \{ \varepsilon \le f\} \le \int _{\alpha } f\, d\mu . \end{equation*}

Dividing by the positive constant \(\varepsilon \) produces the desired bound \(\mu \{ \ell {\lt} u\} \le ((\ell - \theta )^2)^{-1}\int |(u-\theta )_+|^2\, d\mu \).

Theorem 6.27 Geometric closeout for nonlinear recurrences
✓
#

  • DeGiorgi.deGiorgi_recurrence_closeout

Let \(Y_n \ge 0\), \(C {\gt} 0\), \(B {\gt} 1\), \(\alpha {\gt} 0\), and assume

\begin{equation*} Y_{n+1} \le C\, B^n\, Y_n^{1+\alpha }, \qquad Y_0 \le C^{-1/\alpha }\, B^{-1/\alpha ^2}. \end{equation*}

Then \(Y_n \to 0\) as \(n \to \infty \).

Proof ▼

Set \(X := C B^{1/\alpha }\), \(D := X^{1/\alpha }\), \(q := B^{-1/\alpha } {\lt} 1\), and define \(W_n := B^{n/\alpha } Y_n\) and \(Z_n := D W_n\). The hypothesis on \(Y_0\) is equivalent to \(Y_0 \le D^{-1}\), so \(Z_0 \le 1\). From the recurrence

\begin{equation*} W_{n+1} \le X \, W_n^{1+\alpha }, \end{equation*}

multiply by \(D\) and use \(D X = D^{1+\alpha }\) to get \(Z_{n+1} \le Z_n^{1+\alpha }\), hence by induction \(Z_n \le 1\) for all \(n\). This yields \(W_n \le D^{-1}\) and consequently \(Y_n \le D^{-1} q^n\), which tends to \(0\) as \(n \to \infty \).
