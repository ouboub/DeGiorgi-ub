5 Elliptic Coefficients and Weak Formulation

Throughout this section we fix a dimension \(d \in \mathbb {N}\) with \(d \neq 0\) and work in the ambient Euclidean space \(E := \mathbb {R}^d\) (denoted AmbientSpace in the formalization, where it is \(\mathrm{EuclideanSpace}\, \mathbb {R}\, (\mathrm{Fin}\, d)\)). For a \(d \times d\) real matrix \(M\) and \(\xi \in E\), the matrix-vector action is denoted \(M\xi \) (matMulE).

5.1 Elliptic coefficient structure

Definition 5.1
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.EllipticCoeff

Let \(\Omega \subseteq E\). A (nonsymmetric) elliptic coefficient field on \(\Omega \) consists of:

  • a matrix-valued field \(a : E \to \mathbb {R}^{d \times d}\) with each entry \(x \mapsto a(x)_{ij}\) measurable;

  • a lower ellipticity constant \(\lambda {\gt} 0\) (lam);

  • an upper ellipticity constant \(\Lambda \) with \(\lambda \leq \Lambda \);

  • a coercivity statement: for almost every \(x \in \Omega \),

    \begin{equation*} \lambda \, \| \xi \| ^{2} \leq \langle \xi , a(x)\xi \rangle \quad \text{for all } \xi \in E; \end{equation*}

  • an inverse coercivity statement: for almost every \(x \in \Omega \),

    \begin{equation*} \Lambda ^{-1}\, \| \xi \| ^{2} \leq \langle \xi , a(x)^{-1}\xi \rangle \quad \text{for all } \xi \in E. \end{equation*}

✓

For a.e. \(x \in \Omega \) and all \(\xi \in E\),

\begin{equation*} \| a(x)\xi \| ^{2} \leq \Lambda \, \langle \xi , a(x)\xi \rangle . \end{equation*}

Since \(a(x)^{-1}\eta = \xi \) by the previous lemma, the right-hand side equals \(\Lambda \langle \eta , \xi \rangle = \Lambda \langle \xi , a(x)\xi \rangle \).

Lemma 5.7
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.EllipticCoeff.quadratic_upper

For a.e. \(x \in \Omega \) and all \(\xi \in E\),

\begin{equation*} \langle \xi , a(x)\xi \rangle \leq \Lambda \, \| \xi \| ^{2}. \end{equation*}

If \(q = 0\) the bound is trivial. Otherwise multiply \(q \leq nm\) by \(q\) to obtain \(q^{2} \leq n^{2} m^{2} \leq \Lambda n^{2} q\), and divide by \(q {\gt} 0\).

Lemma 5.8
✓

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.EllipticCoeff.mixed_bound

For a.e. \(x \in \Omega \) and all \(\xi , \zeta \in E\),

\begin{equation*} |\langle \zeta , a(x)\xi \rangle | \leq \Lambda \, \| \zeta \| \, \| \xi \| . \end{equation*}

Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(|\langle \zeta , a(x)\xi \rangle | \leq \| \zeta \| \, \| a(x)\xi \| \), so it suffices to show \(\| a(x)\xi \| \leq \Lambda \| \xi \| \). Squaring, \(\| a(x)\xi \| ^{2} \leq \Lambda \langle \xi , a(x)\xi \rangle \leq \Lambda ^{2}\| \xi \| ^{2}\) by the previous two lemmas, and taking square roots (using nonnegativity of both sides) gives the desired estimate.

Let \(d \geq 2\). Let \(\Omega \subseteq E\) be open and bounded, \(A\) an elliptic coefficient field on \(\Omega \), and \(\mathrm{rhs}\) a functional on \(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega )\) that is real-linear and bounded:

\begin{equation*} |\mathrm{rhs}(v)| \leq C_{\mathrm{rhs}}\, \| \nabla v\| _{L^{2}}. \end{equation*}

Then there exists \(u \in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega )\) that is a weak solution of \((\Omega , A, \mathrm{rhs})\).

Proof ▼

Let \(\mu := \mathrm{vol}\! \restriction \! \Omega \) and let \(S = S(\Omega ) \leq L^{2}(\Omega ; E)\) be the smooth-gradient submodule with closure \(H = H(\Omega )\). By the Poincaré inequality on bounded domains in dimension \(d \geq 2\), there is a constant \(C_P {\gt} 0\) with

\begin{equation*} \| u\| _{L^{2}} \leq C_P\, \| \nabla u\| _{L^{2}} \quad \text{for every smooth test } u \text{ on } \Omega . \end{equation*}

For each \(g \in S\) choose, via Classical choice, a smooth test \(u_g\) with \([\nabla u_g] = g\). The Poincaré inequality, lifted via \(\mathrm{toReal}\), gives \(\| [u_g]\| _{L^{2}} \leq C_P \| g\| \), and shows that two such choices with the same gradient class differ by a function with vanishing \(L^{2}\)-class.

Define linear maps on \(S\) by

\begin{equation*} L_0(g) := \mathrm{rhs}(u_g), \qquad U_0(g) := [u_g] \in L^{2}(\Omega ; \mathbb {R}). \end{equation*}

The hypotheses on \(\mathrm{rhs}\) and the Poincaré inequality, together with the fact that two representatives with the same gradient class give the same value of \(\mathrm{rhs}\) (because their difference has zero gradient class and the bound forces \(|\mathrm{rhs}(u_1) - \mathrm{rhs}(u_2)| \leq C_{\mathrm{rhs}} \cdot 0 = 0\)), make \(L_0\) and \(U_0\) well-defined linear maps with bounds

\begin{equation*} |L_0(g)| \leq C_{\mathrm{rhs}}\| g\| , \qquad \| U_0(g)\| \leq C_P\| g\| . \end{equation*}

Since the inclusion \(S \hookrightarrow H\) has dense range (as \(H\) is the closure of \(S\)), \(L_0\) and \(U_0\) extend uniquely to continuous maps \(L : H \to \mathbb {R}\), \(U : H \to L^{2}(\Omega ;\mathbb {R})\).

By the coercivity of the submodule bilinear form established above (\(B_{A,H}\) is bounded and coercive on the Hilbert space \(H\), which is complete; see coeffBilinSubmodule_coercive), the Lax–Milgram theorem produces a unique \(g_{\mathrm{sol}} \in H\) with

\begin{equation*} B_{A,H}(g_{\mathrm{sol}}, w) = L(w) \quad \text{for all } w \in H. \end{equation*}

Set \(G_{\mathrm{sol}}\) to be the underlying \(L^{2}(\Omega ;E)\) element of \(g_{\mathrm{sol}}\) and let \(u_{\mathrm{Lp}} := U(g_{\mathrm{sol}}) \in L^{2}(\Omega ;\mathbb {R})\) with representative \(u : E \to \mathbb {R}\).

Approximate \(g_{\mathrm{sol}}\) by a sequence \(G_n \in S\) (closure), with corresponding smooth tests \(\psi _n\) such that \([\nabla \psi _n] = G_n\). By continuity of \(U\), \([\psi _n] \to u_{\mathrm{Lp}}\) in \(L^{2}\). By the componentwise convergence lemma, the components of \(\nabla \psi _n\) converge to the corresponding components of \(G_{\mathrm{sol}}\) in \(L^{2}\), and \(\psi _n \to u\) in \(L^{2}\). By the standard transition principle for weak partial derivatives under \(L^{p}\)-approximation, \(u\) has weak gradient given by \(G_{\mathrm{sol}}\), providing a witness \(h_u\) with \([\nabla u] = G_{\mathrm{sol}}\). Since \(\psi _n\) are smooth tests on \(\Omega \), \(u \in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega )\).

For any smooth test \(\varphi \) on \(\Omega \) with corresponding \(g_\varphi \in S\),

\begin{equation*} \mathfrak {a}_A(u, \varphi ) = \langle T_A [\nabla u], [\nabla \varphi ]\rangle _{L^{2}} = B_{A,H}(g_{\mathrm{sol}}, g_\varphi ) = L(g_\varphi ) = L_0(g_\varphi ) = \mathrm{rhs}(\varphi ). \end{equation*}

Finally, by approximation: any \(v \in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega )\) admits an approximating sequence \(\varphi _n\) of smooth tests in the \(H^{1}_{0}\)-sense; the bilinear form is continuous against gradient seminorms, and \(\mathrm{rhs}\) is also bounded against the gradient seminorm. Passing to the limit in \(\mathfrak {a}_A(u, \varphi _n) = \mathrm{rhs}(\varphi _n)\) (using independence of the bilinear form on the choice of witness in the right slot) gives \(\mathfrak {a}_A(u, v) = \mathrm{rhs}(v)\) for any witness of \(v\). This completes the proof.
