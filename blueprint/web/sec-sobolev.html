2 Sobolev Spaces and Weak Derivatives

Throughout this section we fix a positive integer \(d\) and write \(E = \mathbb {R}^d\) (in the Lean formalization, \(E\) is the Euclidean space of dimension \(d\), with the canonical orthonormal basis \((e_i)_{i=1}^d\)). All open sets \(\Omega \subseteq E\) are equipped with Lebesgue measure. For a smooth scalar test function \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty (E)\), \(i \in \{ 1,\ldots ,d\} \) and \(x \in E\) we abbreviate the \(i\)-th partial derivative by \(\partial _i \varphi (x) = (D\varphi (x))(e_i)\).

2.1 Weak partial derivatives and weak gradients

Definition 2.1
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.HasWeakPartialDeriv

Let \(\Omega \subseteq E\) be a set, \(i \in \{ 1,\ldots ,d\} \), and \(f, g : E \to \mathbb {R}\). We say that \(g\) is the weak \(i\)-th partial derivative of \(f\) on \(\Omega \), written \(\partial _i^w f = g\) on \(\Omega \), if for every test function \(\varphi \in C^\infty (E,\mathbb {R})\) with compact support and \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subseteq \Omega \) we have

\begin{equation*} \int _\Omega f(x) \, \partial _i \varphi (x) \, dx = - \int _\Omega g(x) \, \varphi (x) \, dx. \end{equation*}

A vector field \(G : E \to E\) is a weak gradient of \(f\) on \(\Omega \), written \(\nabla ^w f = G\), if each component \(G_i\) is the weak \(i\)-th partial derivative of \(f\) on \(\Omega \).

A scalar function \(g : E \to \mathbb {R}\) is a weak divergence of a vector field \(F : E \to E\) on \(\Omega \) if for every \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty \) with \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subseteq \Omega \)

\begin{equation*} \int _\Omega \sum _{i=1}^d F_i(x) \, \partial _i \varphi (x) \, dx = - \int _\Omega g(x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx. \end{equation*}
Lemma 2.3 Uniqueness
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.1

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.HasWeakPartialDeriv.ae_eq

Let \(\Omega \subseteq E\) be open, \(i \in \{ 1,\ldots ,d\} \) and \(f, g_1, g_2 : E \to \mathbb {R}\) with \(g_1\) and \(g_2\) locally integrable on \(\Omega \). If both \(g_1\) and \(g_2\) are weak \(i\)-th partial derivatives of \(f\) on \(\Omega \), then \(g_1 = g_2\) almost everywhere on \(\Omega \).

Proof ▼

It suffices to show that \(g_1 - g_2 = 0\) a.e. on \(\Omega \). The function \(g_1 - g_2\) is locally integrable on \(\Omega \), and for any \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty (E)\) with \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subseteq \Omega \) we have, subtracting the two test identities,

\begin{equation*} \int _\Omega g_1(x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx = \int _\Omega g_2(x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx, \end{equation*}

so \(\int _\Omega (g_1 - g_2)(x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx = 0\). Since \(\varphi \) vanishes outside its support which lies in \(\Omega \), this is the same as integrating over the whole space \(E\). By the standard fact that a locally integrable function whose integral against every smooth compactly supported test function vanishes is zero almost everywhere (IsOpen.ae_eq_zero_of_integral_contDiff_smul_eq_zero), we conclude \(g_1 = g_2\) a.e. on \(\Omega \).

Lemma 2.4 Classical \(\Rightarrow \) weak derivative
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.1

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.HasWeakPartialDeriv.of_contDiff

Let \(\Omega \subseteq E\) be open and \(f \in C^1(E,\mathbb {R})\). Then \(\partial _i f\) is the weak \(i\)-th partial derivative of \(f\) on \(\Omega \).

Proof ▼

Take any \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty (E)\) with \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subseteq \Omega \). Both \(f \cdot \partial _i \varphi \) and \(\partial _i f \cdot \varphi \) vanish outside \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subseteq \Omega \), so the corresponding integrals on \(\Omega \) and on \(E\) coincide. The classical integration-by-parts formula (integral_mul_fderiv_eq_neg_fderiv_mul_of_integrable) for differentiable functions with compactly supported product gives

\begin{equation*} \int _E f(x)\, \partial _i \varphi (x)\, dx = - \int _E (\partial _i f)(x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx, \end{equation*}

which is the weak-derivative identity on \(\Omega \).

Lemma 2.5 Product rule against a smooth factor
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.1

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.HasWeakPartialDeriv.mul_smooth

Let \(\Omega \subseteq E\) be open, \(i \in \{ 1,\ldots ,d\} \), and let \(f, g, \eta : E \to \mathbb {R}\) with \(\eta \in C^\infty \), \(f\) and \(g\) locally integrable on \(\Omega \), and \(g\) a weak \(i\)-th partial derivative of \(f\) on \(\Omega \). Then

\begin{equation*} x \mapsto \eta (x)\, g(x) + (\partial _i \eta (x))\, f(x) \end{equation*}

is the weak \(i\)-th partial derivative of \(\eta f\) on \(\Omega \).

Proof ▼

Fix a test function \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty (E)\) with \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subseteq \Omega \). Then \(\eta \varphi \) is also smooth, compactly supported, with support contained in \(\Omega \). Applying the weak-derivative identity for \(f\) to the test function \(\eta \varphi \) and using the smooth product rule \(\partial _i(\eta \varphi ) = \eta \, \partial _i\varphi + \varphi \, \partial _i\eta \) gives

\begin{equation*} \int _\Omega f(x)\bigl(\eta (x)\, \partial _i\varphi (x) + \varphi (x)\, \partial _i\eta (x)\bigr)\, dx = -\int _\Omega g(x)\, \eta (x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx. \end{equation*}

The local integrability of \(f\) and \(g\), together with continuity of \(\eta \), \(\partial _i\eta \), \(\varphi \), \(\partial _i\varphi \) and compactness of supports, ensures that all integrals are absolutely convergent and may be split. Splitting the left-hand side and rearranging yields

\begin{align*} \int _\Omega \eta (x)\, f(x)\, \partial _i\varphi (x)\, dx & = -\int _\Omega g(x)\, \eta (x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx - \int _\Omega f(x)\, \varphi (x)\, \partial _i\eta (x)\, dx \\ & = -\int _\Omega \bigl(\eta (x)g(x) + (\partial _i\eta (x))f(x)\bigr)\, \varphi (x)\, dx, \end{align*}

which is the desired weak-derivative identity for \(\eta f\).

Lemma 2.6 Restriction to a sub-open set

Let \(\Omega ' \subseteq \Omega \subseteq E\) with \(\Omega '\) open. If \(g\) is the weak \(i\)-th partial derivative of \(f\) on \(\Omega \), then it is also the weak \(i\)-th partial derivative of \(f\) on \(\Omega '\).

Let \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty (E)\) with \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subseteq \Omega '\). The result follows.

Lemma 2.7 \(L^p\)-closure of weak partial derivatives
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.1

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.HasWeakPartialDeriv.of_eLpNormApprox_p

Let \(\Omega \subseteq E\) be open and \(1 {\lt} p {\lt} \infty \). Suppose \(f, g \in L^{p}(\Omega )\) and there are sequences \((\psi _n)\) and \((g_{\psi _n})\) such that:

  • \(g_{\psi _n}\) is the weak \(i\)-th partial derivative of \(\psi _n\) on \(\Omega \) for every \(n\);

  • \(\psi _n - f\) and \(g_{\psi _n} - g\) lie in \(L^{p}(\Omega )\) and tend to \(0\) in \(L^{p}(\Omega )\) as \(n \to \infty \).

Then \(g\) is the weak \(i\)-th partial derivative of \(f\) on \(\Omega \).

Proof ▼

Fix \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty (E)\) with \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subseteq \Omega \). Let \(q\) denote the Hölder conjugate of \(p\), so that \(1 {\lt} q {\lt} \infty \) and \(1/p + 1/q = 1\). Both \(\varphi \) and \(\partial _i\varphi \) are continuous, compactly supported, and hence belong to \(L^{q}(\Omega )\).

By Hölder’s inequality on \((\Omega , dx)\),

\begin{align*} \left| \int _\Omega \partial _i\varphi (x) (\psi _n(x) - f(x))\, dx \right| & \le \| \partial _i\varphi \| _{L^{q}(\Omega )}\, \| \psi _n - f\| _{L^{p}(\Omega )},\\ \left| \int _\Omega \varphi (x) (g_{\psi _n}(x) - g(x))\, dx \right| & \le \| \varphi \| _{L^{q}(\Omega )}\, \| g_{\psi _n} - g\| _{L^{p}(\Omega )}, \end{align*}

both of which tend to zero by hypothesis. Hence

\begin{align*} \int _\Omega \psi _n(x)\, \partial _i\varphi (x)\, dx & \; \to \; \int _\Omega f(x)\, \partial _i\varphi (x)\, dx, \\ \int _\Omega g_{\psi _n}(x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx & \; \to \; \int _\Omega g(x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx. \end{align*}

By assumption, for every \(n\)

\begin{equation*} \int _\Omega \psi _n(x)\, \partial _i\varphi (x)\, dx = -\int _\Omega g_{\psi _n}(x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx, \end{equation*}

and passing to the limit yields

\begin{equation*} \int _\Omega f(x)\, \partial _i\varphi (x)\, dx = -\int _\Omega g(x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx, \end{equation*}

which is the weak-derivative identity for \(f\) and \(g\).

2.2 Sobolev membership and weak-gradient witnesses

Definition 2.8

Let \(p \in [1,\infty ]\), \(\Omega \subseteq E\) a set, and \(f : E \to \mathbb {R}\). We say that \(f \in W^{1,p}(\Omega )\) if \(f \in L^{p}(\Omega )\) and for every \(i \in \{ 1,\ldots ,d\} \) there exists \(g_i \in L^{p}(\Omega )\) such that \(g_i\) is the weak \(i\)-th partial derivative of \(f\) on \(\Omega \).

Definition 2.9
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 2.2

A \(W^{1,p}\)-witness for \(f\) on \(\Omega \) is a tuple consisting of a proof that \(f \in L^{p}(\Omega )\), a vector field \(G : E \to E\) (the weak gradient field) such that each component \(x \mapsto G_i(x)\) lies in \(L^{p}(\Omega )\), together with a proof that \(G\) is a weak gradient of \(f\) on \(\Omega \) (Definition 2.2).

The Hilbert-space case \(p = 2\) is also denoted \(H^1(\Omega ) := W^{1,2}(\Omega )\) in the formalization (DeGiorgi.MemH1).

We say that \(f \in W_0^{1,p}(\Omega )\) if \(f \in W^{1,p}(\Omega )\) and there exist a \(W^{1,p}\)-witness \((G)\) for \(f\) on \(\Omega \) and a sequence \((\varphi _n)_{n\in \mathbb {N}}\) of smooth compactly supported functions with \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi _n \subseteq \Omega \) such that

\begin{equation*} \| \varphi _n - f\| _{L^{p}(\Omega )} \to 0 \quad \text{and}\quad \| \partial _i \varphi _n - G_i\| _{L^{p}(\Omega )} \to 0 \; \text{ for every } i. \end{equation*}

Lemma 2.13 Sum of witnesses
✓
#

Uses

For \(u, v \in H^1(\Omega )\) with witnesses \(G_u\) and \(G_v\), the function \(u + v\) admits the witness \(G_u + G_v\).

Proof ▼

Membership of \(u+v\) in \(L^{2}(\Omega )\) and of \(G_u + G_v\) in \(L^{2}(\Omega )\) in each coordinate follows from \(L^{2}\) being a vector space. To verify the weak-gradient identity, take \(\varphi \in C_c^\infty (E)\) with \(\operatorname{supp}\varphi \subseteq \Omega \). The witnesses \(G_u\) and \(G_v\) give

\begin{align*} \int _\Omega u(x)\, \partial _i\varphi (x)\, dx & = -\int _\Omega (G_u)_i(x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx, \\ \int _\Omega v(x)\, \partial _i\varphi (x)\, dx & = -\int _\Omega (G_v)_i(x)\, \varphi (x)\, dx. \end{align*}

Adding these identities, using local integrability and the linearity of the integral, yields the weak-gradient identity for \(u + v\) with weak gradient \(G_u + G_v\).

Let \(1 \le p \le \infty \), \(\Omega \) open, \(u\) a function with \(W^{1,p}\)-witness \(G\) on \(\Omega \), and \(\eta \in C^\infty (E)\) satisfying \(|\eta (x)| \le C_0\) and \(\| D\eta (x)\| \le C_1\) for all \(x\) and constants \(C_0, C_1 \ge 0\). Then \(\eta u\) admits the \(W^{1,p}\)-witness whose gradient field is

\begin{equation*} x \mapsto \eta (x)\, G(x) + \bigl(\partial _1\eta (x)\, u(x),\ldots ,\partial _d\eta (x)\, u(x)\bigr). \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

The \(L^{p}\) bound on \(\eta u\) follows from the pointwise inequality \(|\eta (x)u(x)| \le C_0 |u(x)|\) and \(u \in L^{p}(\Omega )\). For each component of the displayed gradient field:

  • \(|\eta (x)G_i(x)| \le C_0 |G_i(x)|\), and \(G_i \in L^{p}(\Omega )\);

  • \(|\partial _i\eta (x)\, u(x)| \le \| D\eta (x)\| \, |u(x)| \le C_1 |u(x)|\), and \(u \in L^{p}(\Omega )\).

By Minkowski, the sum lies in \(L^{p}(\Omega )\). The weak-gradient identity is the product rule (Lemma 2.5) applied componentwise to the weak partial derivative \(G_i\) of \(u\) and the smooth factor \(\eta \).

The space \(H_0^1(\Omega )\) is closed under addition, scalar multiplication, and subtraction.

Lemma 2.21 Smooth cutoff between a compact set and an open neighborhood
✓
#

(Formalized as private declaration DeGiorgi.exists_smooth_cutoff.)

Let \(K \subseteq \Omega \subseteq E\) with \(K\) compact and \(\Omega \) open. Then there exists \(\eta \in C^\infty (E)\) with compact support such that \(0 \le \eta \le 1\), \(\eta = 1\) on \(K\), and \(\operatorname{supp}\eta \subseteq \Omega \).

Lemma 2.24 Support of a convolution

Proposition 2.25 \(L^{p}\)-contraction of normed convolution
✓
#

(Formalized as private declaration DeGiorgi.eLpNorm_normedConvolution_le.)

Let \(\rho \in C_c^\infty (E)\) with \(\rho \ge 0\) and \(\int _E \rho = 1\), and let \(1 \le p {\lt} \infty \). Then for every \(f \in L^{p}(E)\),

\begin{equation*} \| \rho * f\| _{L^{p}(E)} \le \| f\| _{L^{p}(E)}. \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

Define the probability measure \(\nu \) on \(E\) by \(d\nu = \rho \, d\, \mathrm{vol}\), which is a probability measure since \(\rho \ge 0\) and \(\int \rho = 1\). For each \(x \in E\),

\begin{equation*} (\rho * f)(x) = \int _E f(x - y)\, d\nu (y). \end{equation*}

By the integral form of Jensen’s inequality applied to the convex function \(t \mapsto t^p\) on \([0,\infty )\) (ConvexOn.map_integral_le),

\begin{equation*} |(\rho * f)(x)|^p \le \left(\int _E |f(x-y)|\, d\nu (y)\right)^p \le \int _E |f(x-y)|^p \, d\nu (y) = (\rho * |f|^p)(x). \end{equation*}

Integrating in \(x\) and using Fubini together with \(\int \rho = 1\),

\begin{equation*} \int _E |(\rho * f)(x)|^p\, dx \le \int _E (\rho * |f|^p)(x)\, dx = \left(\int _E \rho \right)\left(\int _E |f|^p\right) = \| f\| _{L^{p}}^p. \end{equation*}

Taking \(p\)-th roots gives the claim.

Lemma 2.27 Convolution by a shrinking bump approximates a continuous function
✓
#

(Formalized as private declaration DeGiorgi.tendsto_eLpNorm_normedConvolution_sub_of_continuous.)

Let \(g \in C_c(E)\) and \(1 \le p {\lt} \infty \). Then

\begin{equation*} \| \rho _n * g - g\| _{L^{p}(E)} \; \to \; 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty . \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

Choose \(S := \{ x : \operatorname{dist}(x, \operatorname{supp}g) \le 1\} \) and \(T := \{ x : \operatorname{dist}(x, \operatorname{supp}g) \le 2\} \), both compact, with \(S \subseteq T\). The function \(g\) is uniformly continuous on the compact set \(T\). Fix \(\varepsilon {\gt} 0\) and let \(\varepsilon _0 := \varepsilon / (\mathrm{vol}(S)^{1/p} + 1)\). By uniform continuity, there exists \(\delta _0 {\gt} 0\) such that \(|g(y) - g(x)| {\lt} \varepsilon _0\) whenever \(x, y \in T\) with \(\operatorname{dist}(x, y) {\lt} \delta _0\). Set \(\delta := \min (\delta _0, 1)\).

For \(n\) large enough that \(r_{\text{out}}^{(n)} {\lt} \delta \), we have \(\operatorname{supp}(\rho _n * g) \subseteq S\) (Lemma 2.24, since \(r_{\text{out}}^{(n)} \le 1\)). Pointwise, for \(x \in S\),

\begin{equation*} |(\rho _n * g)(x) - g(x)| = \left|\int _E \rho _n(y) (g(x - y) - g(x))\, dy\right| \le \varepsilon _0, \end{equation*}

since \(\rho _n\) is supported in \(B_{r_{\text{out}}^{(n)}} \subseteq B_{\delta }\) and both \(x, x - y \in T\) for \(y \in \operatorname{supp}\rho _n\). For \(x \notin S\) both \((\rho _n * g)(x)\) and \(g(x)\) vanish.

The bound \(\| \rho _n * g - g\| _{L^{p}(E)} \le \varepsilon _0 \, \mathrm{vol}(S)^{1/p}\) then follows from eLpNorm_sub_le_of_dist_bdd, and the choice of \(\varepsilon _0\) gives this is less than \(\varepsilon \).

Theorem 2.28 Convolution by a shrinking bump approximates an \(L^{p}\) function
✓
#

(Formalized as private declaration DeGiorgi.tendsto_eLpNorm_normedConvolution_sub.)

Let \(1 \le p {\lt} \infty \) and \(f \in L^{p}(E)\). Then

\begin{equation*} \| \rho _n * f - f\| _{L^{p}(E)} \; \to \; 0 \quad \text{as } n \to \infty . \end{equation*}

Let \(\Omega \subseteq E\), \(\sigma : E \to \mathbb {R}\) be a.e. measurable on \(\Omega \) and \(g \in L^{2}(\Omega )\). Then the function

\begin{equation*} x \mapsto \mathbf{1}_{\{ \sigma {\gt} 0\} }(x)\, g(x) \end{equation*}

also lies in \(L^{2}(\Omega )\).

Proof ▼

The set \(\{ \sigma {\gt} 0\} \) is measurable since \(\sigma \) is a.e. measurable, so the indicator multiplication preserves a.e. strong measurability. Pointwise, \(|\mathbf{1}_{\{ \sigma {\gt} 0\} }(x)\, g(x)| \le |g(x)|\), and squaring, integrating and applying the \(L^{2}\) membership of \(g\) gives the conclusion.
