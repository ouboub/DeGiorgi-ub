3 Functional Inequalities

This section gathers the functional analytic inequalities used throughout the De Giorgi machinery: a representation–formula proof of the Poincaré inequality on the unit ball, the Sobolev–Poincaré refinement obtained via extension and Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev, the Stampacchia truncation lemma, and the BMO / John–Nirenberg / Campanato package underlying the oscillation theory. Throughout the section, \(E = \mathbb {R}^d\) with \(d \ge 1\) (in most statements \(d \ge 2\), supplied via a NeZero hypothesis), and \(B_{r}\) denotes the open ball of radius \(r\) centred at the origin.

For a smooth scalar function \(u : E \to \mathbb {R}\), the pointwise Euclidean gradient norm is

\begin{equation*} \lvert \nabla u (x)\rvert := \Bigl(\sum _{i=1}^d \bigl(\partial _i u(x)\bigr)^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2}. \end{equation*}

Auxiliary support lemmas smoothGradNorm_eq_zero_of_notMem_tsupport and support_smoothGradNorm_subset_tsupport record that the gradient vanishes outside the closed support of \(u\).

Lemma 3.8 Representation formula
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.representation_formula_smooth

Let \(u \in C^{\infty }\) and let \(\bar u = \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1}} u\). For every \(x \in B_{1}\),

\begin{equation*} \lvert u(x) - \bar u\rvert \le \frac{2^{d}}{d\, \mathrm{vol}(B_{1})}\, \int _{B_{1}} \lvert \nabla u(y)\rvert \, \lvert x - y\rvert ^{1-d}\, dy. \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

For \(\omega \in S^{d-1}\) and \(r\) such that \(x + r\omega \in B_{1}\) one writes \(u(x+r\omega ) - u(x) = \int _{0}^{r} \partial _{\omega } u(x + s\omega )\, ds\) and integrates in \(r\) along radial segments inside \(B_{1}\). Averaging this identity over \(\omega \) weighted by the chord length, and using star_shaped_polar_identity to convert the spherical integral into a weighted volume integral with weight \(\lvert x - y\rvert ^{1-d}\), gives the stated pointwise estimate. The constant \(2^{d}/(d\, \mathrm{vol}(B_{1}))\) comes from estimating the maximal chord length of \(B_{1}\) from any point \(x \in B_{1}\) by \(2\).

Let \(p \ge 1\), let \(w \ge 0\) be a weight on a measurable set \(S\) with \(W := \int _S w\, dy\) finite, and let \(f \ge 0\). Then

\begin{equation*} \Bigl(\int _S f\, w\, dy\Bigr)^{p} \le W^{p-1}\, \int _S f^{p}\, w\, dy. \end{equation*}

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.poincare_smooth_unitBall

Let \(p \ge 1\) and \(u \in C^{\infty }(E,\mathbb {R})\). Then

\begin{equation*} \Bigl\lVert u - \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1}} u \Bigr\rVert _{L^{p}(B_{1})} \le C_{\mathrm{poinc}}(d)\, \bigl\lVert \lvert \nabla u\rvert \bigr\rVert _{L^{p}(B_{1})}. \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

Set \(B = B_{1}\), \(\bar u = \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _B u\), \(f = u - \bar u\), \(g = \lvert \nabla u\rvert \), \(K(z) = \lvert z\rvert ^{1-d}\) and

\begin{equation*} h(x) = \int _B g(y)\, K(x-y)\, dy,\qquad M = d\, \mathrm{vol}(B)\cdot 2,\qquad C_{\mathrm{rep}} = \frac{2^{d}}{d\, \mathrm{vol}(B)}. \end{equation*}

The representation formula yields \(\lvert f(x)\rvert \le C_{\mathrm{rep}}\, h(x)\) pointwise on \(B\). Therefore \(\lVert f\rVert _{L^{p}(B)} \le C_{\mathrm{rep}}\, \lVert h\rVert _{L^{p}(B)}\).

For the Young-type estimate \(\lVert h\rVert _{L^{p}(B)} \le M\, \lVert g\rVert _{L^{p}(B)}\) we apply the weighted power mean lemma to the kernel weight \(w(y) = K(x-y)\), using the \(L^{1}\) bound \(\int _B K(x-y)\, dy \le M\) provided by riesz_kernel_L1_bound. This gives

\begin{equation*} h(x)^{p} \le M^{p-1}\, \int _B g(y)^{p}\, K(x-y)\, dy. \end{equation*}

Integrating in \(x \in B\), using Fubini and the same kernel bound in the \(x\) variable,

\begin{equation*} \int _B h(x)^{p}\, dx \le M^{p-1}\, \int _B g(y)^{p}\Bigl(\int _B K(x-y)\, dx\Bigr) dy \le M^{p}\, \int _B g(y)^{p}\, dy. \end{equation*}

Taking \(p\)-th roots gives \(\lVert h\rVert _{L^{p}(B)} \le M\, \lVert g\rVert _{L^{p}(B)}\). Combining,

\begin{equation*} \lVert f\rVert _{L^{p}(B)} \le C_{\mathrm{rep}}\, M\, \lVert g\rVert _{L^{p}(B)} = 2^{d+1}\, d\, \lVert g\rVert _{L^{p}(B)} = C_{\mathrm{poinc}}(d)\, \lVert g\rVert _{L^{p}(B)}. \qedhere \end{equation*}

A density argument lifts this to general \(W^{1,p}(B_{1})\) functions.

  • DeGiorgi.sobolev_poincare_unitBall

Let \(1 {\lt} p {\lt} d\) and \(u \in W^{1,p}(B_{1})\) with weak gradient \(G\), and write \(p^{*} = dp/(d-p)\). Then

\begin{equation*} \Bigl\lVert u - \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1}} u \Bigr\rVert _{L^{p^{*}}(B_{1})} \le C_{\mathrm{sobPoinc}}(d,p)\, \lVert \lvert G\rvert \rVert _{L^{p}(B_{1})}. \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

Set \(\bar u = \mathop{⨍}\nolimits _{B_{1}} u\) and \(v = u - \bar u\). By the previous lemma \(v \in W^{1,p}(B_{1})\) with weak gradient \(G\). Let \(\widetilde v = \mathrm{ext}(v) \in W_0^{1,p}\) be the unit-ball extension, which has compact support, agrees with \(v\) on \(B_{1}\) and satisfies the lintegral gradient bound

\begin{equation*} \int \lvert \nabla \widetilde v\rvert ^{p} \le \int _{B_{1}} \lvert G\rvert ^{p} + C_{\mathrm{ubExt\, grad}}(d,p)\, \Bigl(\int _{B_{1}} \lvert v\rvert ^{p} + \int _{B_{1}} \lvert G\rvert ^{p}\Bigr). \end{equation*}

Combining this with Poincaré \(\lVert v\rVert _{L^{p}(B_{1})} \le C_{\mathrm{poinc}}(d)\, \lVert \lvert G\rvert \rVert _{L^{p}(B_{1})}\) and raising to the \(p\)-th power gives

\begin{equation*} \lVert \lvert \nabla \widetilde v\rvert \rVert _{L^{p}(\mathbb {R}^{d})} \le C_{\mathrm{ext\! -\! grad}}(d,p)\, \lVert \lvert G\rvert \rVert _{L^{p}(B_{1})} \end{equation*}

(this is the auxiliary lemma extension_gradient_eLpNorm_bound, which uses the algebraic inequalities \(a^{p}+b^{p} \le (a+b)^{p}\) for \(p\ge 1\) to absorb the constants). The whole-space Gagliardo–Nirenberg–Sobolev inequality applied to \(\widetilde v\) gives

\begin{equation*} \lVert \widetilde v\rVert _{L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb {R}^{d})} \le C_{\mathrm{gns}}(d,p)\, \lVert \lvert \nabla \widetilde v\rvert \rVert _{L^{p}(\mathbb {R}^{d})}. \end{equation*}

Restricting the left side to \(B_{1}\), where \(\widetilde v = v\), and chaining the two displayed estimates with the Poincaré bound yields the conclusion with constant \(C_{\mathrm{sobPoinc}}(d,p) = C_{\mathrm{gns}}(d,p)\, C_{\mathrm{ext\! -\! grad}}(d,p)\).

Lemma 3.22 Du Bois-Reymond fundamental lemma
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.du_bois_reymond

Let \(g \in L^{1}(a,b)\) satisfy \(\int _a^b g\, \varphi ' = 0\) for every test function \(\varphi \in C_c^{\infty }(a,b)\). Then \(g\) is constant a.e.

Proof ▼

Fix \(\psi _0 \in C_c^{\infty }(a,b)\) with \(\int \psi _0 = 1\) and let \(c = \int g\, \psi _0\). For any test \(\eta \) write \(\eta = \eta _0 + \alpha \psi _0\) where \(\alpha = \int \eta \) and \(\int \eta _0 = 0\). Since \(\eta _0\) has zero integral, it is the derivative of a test function \(\varphi \) (antiderivative lemma), so the hypothesis gives \(\int g \eta _0 = 0\), hence \(\int g\, \eta = c\, \int \eta \). Equivalently \(\int (g - c)\eta = 0\) for all test \(\eta \), so by the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations \(g = c\) a.e.

Lemma 3.23 \(W^{1,1}\) AC representative
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.w11_ae_eq_ac_representative

Let \(u, g \in L^{1}(a,b)\) with the weak-derivative relation \(\int u\, \varphi ' = -\int g\, \varphi \) for all \(\varphi \in C_c^{\infty }(a,b)\). Then there is a constant \(C\) such that

\begin{equation*} u(x) = C + \int _a^{x} g(t)\, dt \qquad \text{for a.e. } x \in (a,b). \end{equation*}
Proof ▼

Let \(F(x) = \int _a^{x} g\). By integration by parts for absolutely continuous functions, \(F\) has weak derivative \(g\). Hence \(u - F\) has zero weak derivative, and du Bois-Reymond gives \(u - F = C\) a.e.

  • DeGiorgi.weakGrad_ae_eq_zero_on_zeroSet

Let \(\Omega \subseteq E\) be open and \(u \in W^{1,2}(\Omega )\) with weak gradient \(G : \Omega \to \mathbb {R}^{d}\). Then for each \(i \in \{ 1,\dots ,d\} \), \(G_i(x) = 0\) for a.e. \(x \in \Omega \) with \(u(x) = 0\).

Set \(h_i(x) = \mathbf{1}_{\{ u = 0\} }(x)\, G_i(x)\). We show \(h_i = 0\) a.e. on \(\Omega \) by reducing to the variational fundamental lemma: for any test function \(\psi \in C_c^{\infty }(\Omega )\) the integral \(\int \psi \, h_i\) vanishes. By Fubini’s theorem, the line restrictions of \(u\) along the \(i\)-th coordinate axis lie in \(W^{1,1}\) for a.e. line, with weak derivative the line restriction of \(G_i\). The 1D Stampacchia theorem applied on each line gives \(G_i = 0\) a.e. on \(\{ u = 0\} \) along almost every line. Recombining via Fubini gives the result.

Lemma 3.31 Iteration of one-step decay

Let \(E_{\ell } \subseteq B\) be an antitone family of measurable subsets of a measurable set \(B\) with \(\mathrm{vol}(B) {\lt} \infty \). Suppose there exist \(A {\gt} 0\) and \(0 {\lt} \theta {\lt} 1\) with

3.5 Simple iteration lemma

Lemma 3.34
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 3.27

Lean declarations

  • DeGiorgi.simple_iteration_lemma

Let \(A_{\mathrm{iter}} \ge 0\) and let \(\rho : \mathbb {R}\to \mathbb {R}\) satisfy on \([1/2, 1)\):

  1. \(\rho \ge 0\);

  2. \(\sup _{1/2 \le t {\lt} 1}(1-t)^{2}\, \rho (t) {\lt} \infty \);

  3. for all \(1/2 \le s {\lt} t {\lt} 1\), \(\rho (s) \le \tfrac {1}{2}\rho (t) + A_{\mathrm{iter}}(t-s)^{-2}\).

Then \(\rho (1/2) \le C_{\mathrm{iter}}\, A_{\mathrm{iter}}\).

Proof ▼

Let \(M\) be a non-negative upper bound for \((1-t)^{2}\rho (t)\). With the choice \(t = (3s+1)/4\), one has \(t-s = (1-s)/4\) and \(1-t = 3(1-s)/4\), so multiplying the contraction by \((1-s)^{2}\) and using \((1-s)^{2}\, (t-s)^{-2} = 16\) gives

\begin{equation*} (1-s)^{2}\, \rho (s) \le \tfrac {8}{9}\, (1-t)^{2}\rho (t) + 16\, A_{\mathrm{iter}} \le \tfrac {8}{9}\, M + 16\, A_{\mathrm{iter}}. \end{equation*}

Taking the supremum over \(s\) shows \(M \mapsto \tfrac {8}{9}M + 16 A_{\mathrm{iter}}\) also bounds \((1-t)^{2}\rho (t)\). Iterating \(n\) times gives the bound \(M_n = (8/9)^{n} M + (1-(8/9)^{n})\cdot 144\, A_{\mathrm{iter}}\), which tends to \(144\, A_{\mathrm{iter}}\). Evaluating at \(s = 1/2\) where \((1-s)^{2} = 1/4\), we get \(\rho (1/2) \le 4 M_n \to 576\, A_{\mathrm{iter}} \le C_{\mathrm{iter}}\, A_{\mathrm{iter}}\).

Let \(E_{\ell +A} \subseteq E_{\ell }\) be measurable sets with \(\mathrm{vol}(E_{\ell }) {\lt} \infty \) and let \((B_i)\) be a countable family of pairwise disjoint JN-balls with carriers \(\bigcup _i B_i^{\mathrm{car}} \subseteq E_{\ell }\) and 5-fold enlargements covering \(E_{\ell }\). If
